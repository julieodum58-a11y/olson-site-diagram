① Moisture Deficit

18.9% slope runoff (wall front → stake) — drought stress

An 18.9% slope causes rainfall to drain too quickly for Live Oak roots to absorb. Every rain event leaves the tree functionally drought-stressed. The slope (143" horizontal run from retaining wall front → stake, 27" vertical rise) was created by the 2020 retaining wall construction. Dense turfgrass on the slope consumes what little moisture remains before it reaches tree roots. Additionally, new soil/compost fill was placed over the 84" zone between the root flare and retaining wall front, further altering drainage.

Key metric: 27" rise ÷ 143" run (wall front → stake) × 100 = 18.9% grade (≈ 1:5.3 ratio)

② Past Grade Changes

2020 root damage — 84" (root flare → retaining wall front)

The 2020 retaining wall was placed 84" from the Live Oak root flare. New soil/compost fill was placed over this 84" zone in front of the Live Oak following 2020 construction, likely smothering critical lateral feeder roots. Excavation for the new wall footing within the CRZ caused additional root damage. Field photos from 2002/2003 vs. 2020 confirm the dramatic change in wall proximity. The 143" slope (retaining wall front → stake) begins at the face of the new wall.

Key metric: 84" from root flare → retaining wall front (new soil/compost fill zone). 143" from wall front → stake (slope run, 27" rise).

③ Feeder Root Trap

Turf competes for water on slope

Roots that survived or grew outward past the 2020 retaining wall are now covered by dense turfgrass on an 18.9% slope (143" run from wall front → stake, 27" rise). Turfgrass root systems aggressively intercept soil moisture and nutrients in the upper soil profile. Combined with slope-driven runoff, this creates a chronic and worsening moisture and nutrient deficit in the Live Oak's most accessible root zone.

Key insight: Turf root systems consume moisture before it reaches Live Oak feeder roots at depth

Phase 1 Treatment Plan

Sequential remediation protocol for Live Oak root zone recovery

1

Soil Wetting Agent (Surfactant)

Apply penetrant surfactant solution to entire slope zone (143" run from retaining wall front → stake). Breaks hydrophobic surface tension in compacted soil. Dramatically improves water infiltration from rainfall and irrigation. Reduces the effective slope runoff rate on the 18.9% grade.

2

AirSpade Root Zone Excavation

Pneumatic soil excavation using compressed-air tool in the CRZ. Decompresses soil, removes smothered fill material from 2020 construction zone (84" root flare → retaining wall front, where new soil/compost fill was placed). Allows direct visual assessment of root damage and surviving root architecture. Prepares zone for amendment.

3

Deep Root Soil Injections

High-pressure injection of liquid fertilizer, humic acids, bioactive amendments, and mycorrhizal inoculants directly into the root zone at 18–24" depth. Bypasses the competitive turfgrass root system entirely. Delivers critical nutrition and beneficial biology directly to Live Oak feeder roots.
